    For the nonzero frequencies write the two full products as Ca, Db, where C, D contain their
complete common prime support and

                                      (a, b) = 1,        (ab, CD) = 1.

At each common prime, fix its exact valuation in each plain variable and whether it is supplied
by a slot. Dividing out these valuations adds that prime to the mask of every remaining factor
on the side. A slot that supplied the prime is frozen and removed. This description is valid even
when a slot and one or both plain variables supplied that prime. The allocation is made once
for the coefficient, so the same ideals bi occur in the two terms of Db . An impossible allocation
is a zero term. In particular, a scalar forced to vanish by an old moving zero or a common mask
is not replaced by its absolute upper bound before these support conditions have been imposed.
     Let c, d be the logarithmic norms of C, D, and let p be the logarithmic norm of their common
radical. Let r be the product of common primes whose net exponents are nonzero modulo six;
its logarithmic norm is R. The corresponding character ξr is primitive modulo r. In the Möbius
expansion of the complementary common row mask, write e for the selected divisor and E for
its logarithmic norm. In particular

                                         R ≤ p,         E ≤ p − R.
