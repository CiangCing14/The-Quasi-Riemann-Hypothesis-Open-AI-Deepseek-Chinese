Proof. Differentiating fb inserts xβ , and multiplication by a monomial in ξ differentiates xβ f
before Fourier transformation. The L1 bound for the Fourier transform, the product rule, and
the bound of (1+|ξ|)A by a finite sum of monomials of degrees at most ⌈A⌉ give the first assertion.
For a radial function, r∂r corresponds to (ξ · ∂ξ )/2. Expanding its jth power gives finitely many
polynomial multiples of Fourier derivatives, so the first assertion gives the radial one. A fixed
linear change of coordinates changes only its constants.
    For the Mellin assertion, differentiation under the absolutely convergent integral inserts (−s)j .
On the fixed strip this is bounded by a constant times (1 + |t|)j . Write y = eu and integrate the
Fourier transform of e−σu W (eu ) by parts more than h + j + 1 times. The product rule bounds
the resulting L1 derivatives by the stated weighted logarithmic derivatives, because σ ranges over
a fixed set. For a pure twist the Mellin integrand is MW (−σ − it + iω). Substitute v = t − ω
and use (1 + |v + ω|)h+j ≤ (1 + |v|)h+j (1 + |ω|)h+j . Finally the product rule for a fixed annular
cutoff and the chain rule for Ry give the last statement.

4.4    Growth and logarithmic control for Hecke functions
The disk estimate will be applied only after zeros have been excluded from that disk. The follow-
ing elementary growth bound provides the input for the complex-analytic argument and makes
clear which constants are uniform in the conductor. When ψ is a Hecke character, L(s, ψ) ab-
breviates LF (s, ψ). In this subsection Γ(s) denotes Euler’s gamma function; the finite quadratic
function Γ(c) was confined to the arithmetic identities above.
